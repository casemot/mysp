Since the Hebrew alphabet consists of 22 letters, the two central letters are כ (kaph) and ל (lamed), leaving ten letters before and after them in the alphabet. These two letters are also the first and last letters of both versions of the Law. The number of letters in the first version, as already mentioned, is 579. This number is a semiprime, just as the number of letters in the second version, 667, is.
Surprisingly, the relationship
579 + 88 = 3 × 193 + 88 = p(2) × p(44) + 2 × 44 = p(9) × p(10) = 23 × 29 = 667
links the number of letters in the two versions, particularly in terms of its expression as semiprime numbers.
